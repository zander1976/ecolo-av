## The idea in one line

Every part sits on one of **nine levels**. Sensing goes into **one world model**; **one boss**
decides what to do. Each level has a **champion** and **challengers**, scored the same way
(**the league**), and one word picks the whole set, not 36 switches.

## Problem

| What we see | Why it hurts |
|---|---|
| 36 launch switches (`docs/switches.md`) | no one knows which mix is "the car" |
| The lane driver is a rival driver | lanes take turns with Nav2 through twist_mux; they never guide it |
| Some sensing never reaches the planner | the detector's labels, the voxels (a trial) |
| Who is in charge? | Nav2's trees, our navigator, the behaviour planner |
| The car wiggles under Nav2 in the sim (TODO 124) | a separate problem (below), tested on its own |

## The levels

Ben, 13 Nov: routing "will be there no matter what. If someone has a destination then the car
should know that. That is separate from the drivers that actually drive the car."

```text
 ALWAYS ON, whoever drives
 1 Routing        which roads           campus router (road graph)
 2 Localization   where am I            EKF: GPS, IMU, wheels (+ NDT)
 3 Perception     what's around         lidar, radar, camera boxes, lane lines
 4 World model    where may I go        costmap (walls + soft lanes), and a list of things

 THE "NAV" DRIVER: one of several drivers
 5 Behaviour      the rules             stop, wait, pass, speed cap (a state machine)
 6 Path           the exact line        lattice, diffusion, Hybrid-A*
 7 Follow+avoid   steer along it        MPPI (avoids), RPP, MPC (follow only)

 PICK ONE DRIVER: twist_mux, by priority
    joystick > RC > keyboard > tests > scripted > buttons > replay > point (GPS) > nav > ai

 ALWAYS LAST
 8 Ego            what the car can do   speed + turn -> steering angle; limits, lag
 9 Safety         may it move at all    e-stop, geofence, stop sign, watchdog, firmware
```

- Levels 1-4 serve every driver and the locks. Levels 5-7 are one driver, `nav`.
- Diffusion draws the path (level 6); it doesn't steer.
- Ego is not a state machine: it translates and limits. The behaviour planner is the state machine.
- Level 4 is two views (Fable): a grid can't hold a moving person or a sign's meaning, so the
  boss also reads **a list of things** (each with a kind, a place and a speed).

## Four rules

| # | Rule | In practice |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **One world model** | Every sensor and map writes into the costmap, the list of things and one pose. A new idea becomes a layer, not a new driver |
| 2 | **One boss, with a contract** | The behaviour planner alone decides *what*. Written down: who listens, and what silence means ("no crossing, slow down", never "carry on") |
| 3 | **The league** | Each level has a champion and challengers, in one file |
| 4 | **One scorecard** | Every challenger is scored on the same campus routes and the same recordings |

## The league: picking the best of each level

Ben, 13 Nov: "What can we say about picking the best algorithms in each location maybe and
making this a unified system."

```text
  stacks.yaml (one file, the truth)
  ┌──────────────┬────────────────┬──────────────────────────────┬────────────┐
  │ Level        │ Champion       │ Challengers                  │ Last score │
  ├──────────────┼────────────────┼──────────────────────────────┼────────────┤
  │ Localization │ EKF            │ NDT+EKF, KISS                │ date, link │
  │ Path         │ lattice (Nav2) │ lattice_rs, diffusion        │ date, link │
  │ Follow+avoid │ MPPI (Nav2)    │ mppi_lag, mpc_lag, rpp       │ date, link │
  └──────────────┴────────────────┴──────────────────────────────┴────────────┘
        │                                   │
        ▼                                   ▼
  stack:=best  = every champion       pixi run measure league
  stack:=car   = champions approved     re-scores every challenger on the same
                 for the car              routes and recordings; proposes a swap
```

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| One file names each level's champion | the launch files, docs and screens read it: "what is the car?" has one answer |
| A challenger wins by a margin, on 3 or more runs | 7 of 12 against 8 is a tie, not a loss |
| A person approves each swap; the car needs its own win, on the Orin | a sim win isn't a car win |
| Nothing is deleted | the loser stays a challenger (Ben, 5 Nov) |
| Each stack runs `sim-check` in CI; each recording says which stack drove it | so a score always names its stack (Fable) |

## From 36 switches to 8

Ben, 13 Nov: "Thats to many switches, we want to reduce that for sure." One switch per level,
one word for the whole set. Options are cheap; switches are not.

| Switch | Options | Replaces |
|---|---|---|
| `stack:=` | `best`, `car`, `stock`, `ours` | sets every switch below at once |
| `pose:=` | `ekf`, `ndt_ekf`, `kiss_ekf`; `scored:=ndt,kiss,amcl` | `ndt`, `ndt_ekf`, `kiss_icp`, `kiss_ekf`, `amcl` |
| `costmap:=` | `nav2`, `ours`; `layers:=voxels,lanes` | `costmap`, `voxels` |
| `navigator:=` | `nav2`, `ours` | unchanged (Fable: keep it apart from behaviour) |
| `behaviour:=` | `off`, `on` | unchanged |
| `path:=` | `lattice`, `lattice_rs`, `hybrid`, `diffusion`, `smac2d`, `navfn`, `theta_star` | `planner` (both meanings) |
| `steer:=` | `mppi`, `mppi_lag`, `mpc_lag`, `rpp`, `stanley`, … | `controller`, `tracker` |
| `world:=`, `simulator:=` | unchanged | — |

- `pose:=` keeps apart what **steers** (`pose:=`) from what is only **scored beside** (`scored:=`).
- `steer:=` refuses a choice that can't reverse when `reversing:=true` (Fable).
- Test knobs (`lag_scale`, `sim_speed`, `geofence`, `lidar`) stay, on their own list.
- Old names keep working for one release, with a warning.

## First piece: the lane layer

Lane lines are a **cost, not a wall**. Crossing is allowed, only when staying is worse.
Fable's review changed how:

| Was | Now | Why (checked in Nav2's source) |
|---|---|---|
| one fixed mask from `lanes.yaml` | drawn **for each route**: "right" depends on the way you drive | a two-way road's right lane is the other's left |
| flat bands 0 / 40 / 60 / 120 | a **ramp**, lowest at the right lane's centre | the keepout filter keeps the higher cost (`keepout_filter.cpp:365`); inflation (3 m) puts about 56 on both lane centres, so 40 would be hidden |
| MPPI keeps the lane by cost | **the planner** draws the path in the right lane; MPPI follows the path | MPPI weighs staying on the path about 30 times more than the costmap |

```text
 cost   kerb   edge line        centre line                    edge line   kerb
 wall   ███                                                                ███
 high   ███    ▓▓                                                  ▓▓      ███
        ███       ▒▒                ▒▒▒                       ▒▒          ███
 low    ███          ░░ left lane ░░    ░░                ░░              ███
 lowest ███                                ▁ right lane's centre ▁        ███
```

| Situation | What the car does |
|---|---|
| Road clear | the path sits at the right lane's centre |
| Right lane blocked | the block is a wall; round through the left lane costs less than never arriving |
| The boss says "no passing here" | it raises the left lane's cost |
| The boss goes silent | "no crossing" (the contract) |

| Step | What |
|---|---|
| 1, stock | a small Python node draws the route's ramp from `lanes.yaml` into a mask; Nav2's keepout filter reads it on the **global** costmap |
| 1b, compare | a lane-centre planner (`path:=lane`, Autoware's idea): the path *is* the right lane's centre. Maybe simpler than shaping costs |
| 2, ours | a `lanes` layer in the Rust costmap, with the camera's lines |
| Later | Lanelet2 as the lane map's format, not a growing `lanes.yaml` (licence and build to check) |
